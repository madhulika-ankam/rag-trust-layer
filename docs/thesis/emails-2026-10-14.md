## 1. To Dr. Maier — the framing question

**Subject:** Thesis results and one question on framing — draft chapters Monday

Dear Dr. Maier,

When we last spoke you said the trust scores were not mathematically backed.
I have now built the validation we discussed, and I would like to check that
it answers your objection before I write it up.

**What the validation consists of**

I built a minimal-pair benchmark of 302 claim–evidence pairs from the three
machine manuals in the corpus, following the protocol used in BUMP (Ma et
al., ACL 2023) and FactCC. Each negative pair is a single controlled
corruption of a supported claim — a changed number, an inverted negation, a
swapped equipment code or term, a dropped condition, or an added unsupported
sentence. The set is split 152 validation / 150 test by a fixed seed. Every
threshold and weight is selected on the validation half; the test half is
reported once.

**Results on the held-out test split (n = 150)**

| configuration | balanced accuracy | ROC-AUC |
|---|---|---|
| NLI entailment alone | 0.931 | 0.963 |
| + semantic similarity | 0.931 | 0.959 |
| + entity matching | 0.931 | 0.975 |
| + negation checking (full system) | 0.919 | 0.975 |
| logistic regression, coefficients fitted on validation | 0.931 | 0.976 |

Balanced accuracy follows SummaC (Laban et al., TACL 2022); ROC-AUC follows
TRUE (Honovich et al., NAACL 2022). The hand-set 0.30/0.70 weighting I could
not previously justify turns out to perform within 0.001 AUC of coefficients
fitted by logistic regression on held-out data — so it is no longer an
unexplained constant, it is a choice with a measured alternative beside it.
The decision thresholds are swept rather than chosen, and reported as a
risk–coverage curve following the selective-prediction literature.

**The question I need your view on**

Validating the verifier was the easy half. The harder finding is this.

I labelled 30 system answers by hand, blind to the system's own decision.
Not one of them contained a claim that contradicted the manuals, and
exactly **one** contained content the manuals do not support. The other
problems I found were omission (an answer that drops the manual's
"tolerance of 30 degrees" from a safety condition), incompleteness, and
off-target retrieval — none of which is hallucination under the definition
I am using.

In other words, a retrieval pipeline with a grounding instruction in the
prompt produced roughly one ungrounded answer in thirty on this corpus.

So the trust layer's measurable effect is not that it removes
hallucinations — there was essentially one to remove. Its effect is that it
reliably detects unsupported content when present, at a measurable cost in
answer availability: of 13 answers I judged fully sound, the system
currently delivers 6 and withholds 7.

My plan is to write the thesis around that: **reliable detection, a measured
availability cost, and a diagnosis of where the cost comes from.** The
diagnosis is the part I think may be novel. Verification quality degrades
sharply with the length of the claim being checked — mean entailment 0.68
for claims under 12 words against 0.33 at 20 or more (Spearman rho −0.281,
p = 3e-09) — while semantic similarity over the same claims moves in the
*opposite* direction (rho +0.326), which rules out the obvious explanation
that longer claims are simply less on topic.

What makes me think this is worth reporting is the control condition. On my
perturbation benchmark, where every claim is a single proposition taken
from a manual, the same model at the same setting shows **no degradation at
all** across the same length range (mean entailment 0.87 at under 15 words,
0.91 at 30 or more). So the effect is not the NLI model failing on long
hypotheses; it is a property of how generated answers get decomposed into
claims. The factual-consistency literature varies the length of the
*evidence* — I have not found a paper that reports accuracy against the
length of the *claim*, which is the variable a decomposition module
controls.

**Is that an acceptable result for the thesis, or were you expecting the
layer to show a net improvement in system usefulness?** The two framings need
different Chapter 5s, and I would rather know now than rewrite it.

I am sending Chapters 2 and 3 on Monday 19 October. My defense is 6 November.

With thanks,
Madhulika

## 2. To Dr. Morkos — 20 labels to review

**Subject:** 20 minutes of your time — validating decision labels for my thesis

Dear Dr. Morkos,

I have a small request that would materially strengthen one chapter of my
thesis, and it should take about twenty minutes.

My benchmark has 161 questions about three machine manuals (Bridgeport
knee mill, HAAS lathe, UR5e robot arm). Each one carries a label saying what
a trustworthy system *should* do with it:

- **ANSWER** — the manuals contain the answer; a correct answer should be
  delivered
- **WARN** — the manuals partly support an answer; it should be delivered
  with a caveat
- **ESCALATE / REFUSE** — the manuals do not support an answer; the system
  should withhold it and say so

I generated these labels myself with a rule-based heuristic, and I have no
independent confirmation that the protocol is sound. Until someone with
manufacturing expertise checks them, I have to state in the thesis that the
decision labels are heuristic and that decision accuracy cannot be presented
as validated.

**What I am asking:** would you look at the attached 20 questions and mark
whether you agree with the label? Agree / disagree / unsure is enough — no
need to write an answer to any of the questions. If you disagree on some, the
disagreement is itself useful; I would report inter-annotator agreement
rather than pretend the labels are certain.

The attachment has one row per question with the question text, the relevant
manual, and the current label. The 20 are sampled across all three machines
and across the three label types.

My defense is on 6 November, so anything you can return before the end of
next week would help a great deal. If twenty is too many, even ten would
change what I am able to claim.

With thanks,
Madhulika
